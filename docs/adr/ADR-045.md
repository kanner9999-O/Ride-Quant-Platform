## Context

`ADR-042` (Approved) retired the mandatory-two-independent-review gate,
replacing it with `Executor -> Review A -> Risk Classification (R0/R1/R2)
-> Product Owner Decision -> Execution` — Review A remains the single
mandatory formal technical review; R2 recommends, never requires, an
optional advisory cross-check chosen by the Product Owner. That change
reduced review-gate ceremony but left every governed decision — regardless
of risk class — routed to an explicit Product Owner Decision as the final
step before execution.

Recent Feature Engine `P3-FEATURE-QG-EVID-03` governance traffic (the
`ADR-044`/Chapter 13 §13.8.1 correction chain; four sequential Condition-2
§4.1(b) reclassification candidates, `candidate-002` through `-005`, each
requiring its own Product Owner Decision to apply an *already-approved*
mechanism to a new bounded evidence cohort) demonstrates a recurring
pattern: a large share of Product Owner decision traffic is not a policy,
architecture, or product-direction judgment — it is confirming that an
already-governed, already-approved mechanism was correctly and boundedly
applied to a new, individually-verifiable technical case. Product Owner has
instructed reducing this routine traffic while preserving Product Owner
authority over genuinely important decisions: policy, product direction,
architecture/governance change, approval gates, and meaningful risk.

## Decision question

What governed routing lets Review A close a bounded, already-governed
technical decision directly — without weakening Product Owner authority
over policy, architecture, approval gates, or genuine risk?

## Decision — target principle

```text
Product Owner approves policy, product direction, architecture/governance
change, approval gates, and meaningful risk.

An eligible AI Technical Architect may resolve routine bounded
applications of already-approved policy when strict delegation
eligibility is satisfied.
```

`ADR-045` **supersedes** `ADR-042` (`MAJOR-01` correction). `ADR-042`
fixed a single target workflow — `Executor -> Review A -> Risk
Classification -> Product Owner Decision -> Execution`, with Risk
Classification always followed by a Product Owner Decision — and this ADR
intentionally changes that routing for a closed subset of decisions;
`ADR-045`, not `ADR-042`, is therefore the single current authority for
the combined model once activated. This is not a rewrite of `ADR-042`'s
own Decision: every `ADR-042` semantic that remains valid is carried
forward unchanged (see "Authority compatibility — `ADR-042` supersession
scope" below) — only the step *after* Risk Classification changes, and
only for the closed subset of decisions satisfying `D1`–`D12`.

### Authority compatibility — `ADR-042` supersession scope

`ADR-042`'s entire operative content — Review A's mandatory/independent
status, the R0/R1/R2 definitions, R0/R1's `NO CROSS-CHECK` default, R2's
Product-Owner-only routing, the optional R2 cross-check's advisory/
non-mandatory/non-veto/non-persisted semantics, peer-reviewer no-veto,
and non-retroactivity — remains valid and is carried forward into
`ADR-045` **unchanged in substance**. The only intentional delta is
narrow and explicit:

```text
ADR-042 (superseded routing):
  ... -> Risk Classification -> Product Owner Decision -> Execution
  (unconditional — every decision reaches Product Owner)

ADR-045 (this ADR's routing, once activated):
  ... -> Risk Classification -> routing:
    eligible R0/R1 + D1-D12 satisfied -> DELEGATED TECHNICAL RESOLUTION
    otherwise                          -> Product Owner Decision
```

`ADR-042`'s file is **never modified** — Chapter 11 §11.3 forbids editing
an Approved ADR; `ADR-042.md` stays byte-identical/immutable, exactly as
`ADR-042` itself never modified `ADR-031.md`. Its own definitions (Review
A independence, R0/R1/R2, optional cross-check semantics) are not
declared incorrect and remain the authoritative source of truth for those
unchanged concepts — `ADR-045` restates them here only for this
document's own self-containedness, never as a competing or diverging
definition. Once activated, `ADR-042`'s current lifecycle state becomes
`Superseded` (by `ADR-045`), recorded at MANIFEST per I-12, mirroring the
same relationship `ADR-042` itself established with `ADR-031` — this
transition is described in "Activation" below and is **not performed by
this candidate**.

### Approval — reserved to Product Owner, unchanged

At minimum, all of the following remain exclusively Product Owner
authority; no AI may perform them under this ADR:

```text
- ADR approval/rejection (every ADR, Chapter 11);
- Phase Approval Gate decisions (Chapter 12 §12.2);
- Module/Product approval gate decisions;
- product direction, scope, priority, and value tradeoffs;
- governance or approval-process changes (this ADR itself included);
- Platform Invariant / Event Schema / module-taxonomy/dependency changes;
- any decision requiring an ADR under Chapter 0 §4b;
- every R2 decision;
- explicit risk acceptance (Chapter 0 §3's "Chấp nhận rủi ro" requirement);
- LIVE authorization, if/when a future governing authority permits it;
- any decision a governing artifact explicitly reserves to Product Owner.
```

Delegation narrows routine operational decision traffic. It does not
transfer Product Owner ownership of the product, and does not create a
second approval authority.

### Delegated Technical Resolution — a distinct, narrower concept

A **Delegated Technical Resolution** is a technical governance outcome
reached under already-approved authority. It is explicitly **NOT**:

```text
- an ADR approval;
- a Phase/Module approval;
- product acceptance;
- Product Owner risk acceptance;
- a new architecture decision;
- a new governance rule;
- an Approved/Locked lifecycle transition.
```

The exact terminology `DELEGATED TECHNICAL RESOLUTION` is used everywhere
this outcome is recorded. It is never called "AI approval," and no
artifact created under this model may write `approved_by: AI` or any
fabricated Product Owner decision text.

## Decision — closed delegation eligibility predicate (D1–D12, conjunctive)

A decision may use Delegated Technical Resolution **only when ALL of the
following hold** — no partial satisfaction, no "mostly satisfies":

```text
D1.  ADR Scope Rule = ADR_NOT_REQUIRED.

D2.  Risk Classification is R0 or R1. R2 is NEVER delegated.

D3.  An existing Approved/Locked/EFFECTIVE authority already defines the
     rule/mechanism being applied.

D4.  The transaction only applies that existing rule to a bounded,
     individually-verifiable technical/evidence case.

D5.  It creates no new product semantics, architecture semantics,
     governance semantics, contract semantics, invariant, schema, module
     dependency, or policy.

D6.  It does not alter product scope, priority, roadmap direction,
     user/business acceptance criteria, or a Product-Owner-owned value
     tradeoff.

D7.  It does not accept a new residual risk on behalf of Product Owner.

D8.  Review A is independently performed by an eligible AI Technical
     Architect and returns CLEAN: 0 Blocker / 0 Major. Non-blocking Minor
     findings may remain under existing Semantic Sufficiency rules
     (execution-rules.md).

D9.  The exact evidence/authority/boundary needed for the resolution is
     fresh-verified per G-VERIFY / P3-VERIFY where applicable.

D10. Neither of the following is true (`MINOR-01` correction — both are
     independent, either alone fails this condition):
     (a) a governing artifact explicitly reserves this decision class to
         Product Owner; or
     (b) the Product Owner has explicitly called in/reserved this
         specific decision. No AI may override an explicit Product Owner
         call-in.

D11. The delegated outcome is bounded and deterministic enough that
     another eligible reviewer applying the same authority/evidence
     should reach the same governance disposition.

D12. Delegation eligibility itself is unambiguous. If eligibility is
     uncertain, FAIL CLOSED TO PRODUCT OWNER.
```

## Decision — risk-classified routing

Preserves `ADR-042`'s R0/R1/R2 definitions unchanged; adds routing only:

```text
R0: pure deterministic bookkeeping is not a semantic decision and
    continues under existing G-TXN rules unchanged; if an actual governed
    technical resolution exists at R0 (not mere bookkeeping), it may use
    the delegated lane when D1-D12 pass.

R1: Review A required (unchanged from ADR-042). If Review A returns CLEAN
    (0 Blocker / 0 Major) AND D1-D12 all pass: DELEGATED TECHNICAL
    RESOLUTION — no Product Owner decision required for this decision.

R2: NEVER delegated, no exception. Routes to Product Owner exactly as
    ADR-042 already defines. ADR-042's optional independent cross-check
    semantics at R2 are unchanged by this ADR.
```

Review A independence (Chapter 0 §3, `G-REV-003`) is not weakened by this
ADR in any way — a Delegated Technical Resolution still requires the same
independent, non-inherited verification Review A already performs; this
ADR only changes what happens *after* a genuinely CLEAN, genuinely
eligible R0/R1 verdict.

## Decision — AI Technical Architect delegated authority

When D1–D12 pass, an eligible Review A principal may issue:

```text
DELEGATED TECHNICAL RESOLUTION — CLEAN
```

The same principal must not have been the executor/author of the
underlying work where existing Review-A independence rules already
prohibit that (Chapter 0 §3, `G-REV-003` — unchanged). The result
authorizes: the bounded technical disposition itself; deterministic
state/bookkeeping updates directly caused by it; progression to the next
governed technical step. It does **not** authorize any Product-Owner-
reserved state transition — it cannot set `Approved`, `Locked`, Phase
Approved, Module Approved, or LIVE Authorized, and cannot itself waive an
ADR-Required determination.

## Decision — escalation rules (fail closed to Product Owner)

Escalate to Product Owner if **any** of the following applies:

```text
- Risk Classification resolves to R2;
- ADR Scope Rule resolves to ADR_REQUIRED;
- Review A has any unresolved Blocker/Major;
- a product/business tradeoff exists;
- the architecture/governance mechanism itself must change (as opposed to
  applying an existing one);
- a new contract/invariant/schema/module dependency would be introduced;
- explicit risk acceptance is required;
- the exact governing authority conflicts or is ambiguous;
- delegation eligibility (D1-D12) cannot be proven;
- a governing artifact explicitly reserves the decision to Product Owner
  (`MINOR-01`); or
- the Product Owner has explicitly called in/reserved this specific
  decision (`MINOR-01`) — no AI may override that request.
```

Review A may also voluntarily escalate a nominal R1 case to Product Owner
if the bounded classification understates the case's actual risk — this
mirrors `ADR-042`'s own R1-escalates-to-R2 provision and is never
prohibited by this ADR.

## Decision — persistence model: persist outcome, not ceremony

For a Delegated Technical Resolution, persist only what is needed for
auditability:

```text
- decision/resolution identity;
- exact subject;
- governing authority/mechanism (the already-approved rule applied);
- reviewed boundary;
- Review A principal;
- Review A verdict;
- Risk Classification;
- delegation eligibility = satisfied (D1-D12);
- resulting bounded state transition;
- remaining unresolved items, if any.
```

Do **not** require: fake Product Owner approval text; a long approval
script; optional cross-check evidence for R0/R1 (none exists — cross-check
is R2-only under `ADR-042`, unchanged); redundant ceremony artifacts. No
`approved_by: AI`. No fabricated Product Owner decision of any kind.

## Decision — non-retroactivity and the pending-work rule

Historical completed Product Owner approvals remain valid and are never
rewritten. This model applies **only** to resolution decisions made
*after* `ADR-045`'s own atomic activation.

**Pending-work rule (required so already-authored candidates are not
frozen out, without retroactively rewriting their authoring history):** an
unresolved candidate authored **before** activation MAY use the delegated
model **after** activation if, and only if:

```text
- no Product Owner decision has already completed for it;
- its resolution decision itself occurs after activation;
- its evidence/authority/boundary are fresh-re-verified at the resolution
  boundary (not merely inherited from its authoring-time verification);
- D1-D12 all pass at the resolution boundary.
```

This ADR does not itself resolve any such pending candidate, and does not
predetermine that any specific pending candidate will pass D1–D12 at a
future resolution boundary — that determination is made fresh, at
resolution time, under whatever authority is controlling then.

## Alternatives considered

1. **Status quo — every decision reaches Product Owner regardless of risk
   class.** Rejected — this is precisely the disproportionate routine
   traffic the Product Owner decision direction identifies, evidenced by
   four sequential near-identical §4.1(b) candidate approvals.
2. **Delegate R2 as well, with mandatory cross-check as a substitute
   safeguard.** Rejected — contradicts the Product Owner's explicit
   instruction to preserve Product Owner authority over "meaningful risk,"
   and would silently reintroduce a mandatory-gate failure mode ADR-042
   already retired for a different reason.
3. **A scored/weighted eligibility system instead of a closed predicate.**
   Rejected — mirrors ADR-042's own rejection of "scoring systems, forms,
   evidence registries, or additional ceremony"; a closed, conjunctive
   D1–D12 predicate is simpler to apply and to fail closed on than a
   score.
4. **A new "Delegate" role distinct from `AI Technical Architect`.**
   Rejected — no new role is needed; the existing role's Review A
   eligibility (Chapter 0 §2) already carries the necessary technical
   authority, and a new role would itself be an unnecessary governance
   addition contrary to `G-ADR-001`'s "no gap -> ADR reflex."
5. **Retroactively apply the delegated model to already-completed Product
   Owner decisions.** Rejected — explicitly prohibited by the
   non-retroactivity principle (mirrors `ADR-042` §"historical evidence
   unaffected"); historical decisions keep the approval validity they were
   made under.
6. **Bounded exception-based delegation for R0/R1 only, R2 and all
   PO-reserved classes explicitly excluded (CHOSEN).** Narrowest rule that
   reduces routine traffic without touching any decision class the
   Product Owner instruction identified as requiring continued Product
   Owner authority.

## Consequences

Chapter 0, Global Execution Rules, MANIFEST, or CHANGELOG (see Activation

resolve `Candidate-005` or any other pending decision, does not touch

becomes `Superseded`, per "Authority compatibility" above); historical
decisions keep their existing validity, unreinterpreted.
