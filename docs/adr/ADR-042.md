# ADR-042: Review Gate Simplification — Single Mandatory Review A with Optional Risk-Classified Cross-Check

**Context:** [Chapter 0](../constitution/00-governance.md) §3's "Review gate bắt buộc" and [Chapter 11](../constitution/11-adr-process.md) §11.5/§11.9 currently require a minimum of **two** independent review executions before every Product Owner ADR/approval-gate decision — satisfied either by Mode A (`DISTINCT_PRINCIPAL`) or Mode B (`SAME_PRINCIPAL_DISTINCT_EXECUTION`, with a mandatory execution-isolation evidence contract), per [ADR-031](./ADR-031.md) (Approved). This mandatory-two-review gate applies uniformly to every decision regardless of actual semantic risk — a typo fix and a Platform Invariant change both require the same two-independent-review machinery today. Product Owner has decided to retire this uniform requirement: keep a single mandatory formal technical review (Review A), and make a second independent review a strictly **optional, advisory** cross-check selected by risk classification rather than a blanket gate.

**Ground truth, independently confirmed at this boundary:** `docs/constitution/00-governance.md` §3 (re-read fresh) states the mandatory-two-review rule verbatim ("Trước khi Product Owner quyết một ADR hoặc tài liệu thuộc approval gate, phải có tối thiểu hai independent reviews"), Mode A/Mode B eligibility, and fail-closed behavior when independence cannot be resolved. `docs/constitution/11-adr-process.md` §11.4 (Metadata contract — `reviewers` field semantic), §11.5 (Review and acceptance gate — mirrors Chapter 0 §3), §11.6 (Approval transition must pin reviewer evidence atomically with approval), and §11.9 (Validator contract — mechanically checks "minimum-two eligible independent review EXECUTIONS," Mode A/Mode B resolution, fail-closed on unresolved independence) all currently encode this same mandatory-two-review premise. `docs/adr/ADR-031.md` (Approved v0.2) defines the Principal Identity vs Execution Identity distinction and the Mode A/Mode B execution-isolation evidence contract that exists *specifically* to let a second review be satisfied without a second distinct principal — its own §11 "Migration / Atomic Activation Boundary" establishes the exact atomic-activation-set pattern this ADR reuses by direct citation. `docs/templates/adr-template.md` embeds a two-row `Independent reviews` evidence table with Mode A/Mode B guidance as boilerplate on every new ADR. `docs/governance/execution-rules.md` `G-REV-003` ("Independent Review B PHẢI giữ độc lập THẬT SỰ...") presupposes Independent Review B is itself a mandatory fixture. `docs/governance/phases/phase-3-rules.md` `P3-REVIEW-001`'s review-depth table currently routes "new architecture / authority / contract semantics" to `full governed review (Review A/B)`, and `P3-IDENTITY-001` is an operational pre-check anchored explicitly on "Chapter 0 §3/Chapter 11 §11.5's reviewer-independence authority (Mode A/Mode B)" without redefining it. This session's own transaction history independently corroborates the operational cost this ADR responds to: multiple transactions in this repository were correctly halted specifically because genuine, traceable Independent Review B evidence could not be produced for a given boundary — not because the underlying change was judged high-risk, but because the mandatory-two-review gate applies uniformly regardless of risk.

**Decision question:** what review-gate model keeps Review A's independent technical scrutiny mandatory for every decision while making a second independent review proportional to actual semantic risk instead of uniform ceremony?

**Decision — target workflow:**

```text
Executor -> Review A -> Risk Classification -> Product Owner Decision -> Execution
```

- **Review A remains the single mandatory formal technical review.** Role `AI Technical Architect` eligibility (Chapter 0 §2) is unchanged. Review A must independently inspect the exact candidate/repository authority — it does not inherit or rubber-stamp the Executor's own conclusion (this preserves `G-REV-003`'s actual independence discipline, reframed onto Review A now that it is the sole mandatory review rather than a property specifically of "Independent Review B").
- **Product Owner remains the sole approve/reject authority** (Chapter 0 §2/§3, unchanged) and remains free to request a cross-check for any reason, including reasons outside the R0/R1/R2 taxonomy below.
- **Risk Classification is a new, mandatory step between Review A and Product Owner Decision** — Review A (or, failing that, the Product Owner) classifies the change into exactly one of three risk classes before the Product Owner decides.

**Decision — the three risk classes:**

- **R0 — Mechanical / no semantic risk.** Typo/formatting; deterministic bookkeeping; mechanical factual correction; test-expectation alignment; refactor proven not to alter behavior/contract; generated/index/reference synchronization. Default: `NO CROSS-CHECK`. Use deterministic verification/tests as appropriate instead.
- **R1 — Bounded semantic / normal implementation risk.** Implementation change within one module; bounded bug fix; bounded semantic correction; internal algorithm change that does not alter external contract/authority; reversible change with limited blast radius. Default: `NO CROSS-CHECK` — Review A is sufficient unless Review A or the Product Owner escalates the specific case to R2 because actual uncertainty/risk is materially higher than the nominal category suggests.
- **R2 — High semantic / architectural risk.** Classified when one or more materially applies: Platform Invariant or Event Schema change; authority / source-of-truth semantics; identity, lifecycle, replay, versioning, retention, or activation semantics; cross-module contract/dependency change with significant blast radius; migration difficult or expensive to reverse; destructive/irreversible state transition; Review A finds conflicting authority/evidence or unresolved ambiguity; Review A's own confidence is materially low; repeated semantic correction on the same artifact indicates the root cause may still be misunderstood; or the Product Owner explicitly requests a second opinion. R2 behavior: `RECOMMEND OPTIONAL INDEPENDENT CROSS-CHECK` — **R2 MUST NOT automatically require the cross-check.** For an R2 case, Review A must briefly tell the Product Owner (a) why the case is R2, and (b) what specific uncertainty/risk a cross-check could reduce. The Product Owner then chooses `CROSS-CHECK` or `PROCEED WITHOUT CROSS-CHECK`. **Absence of a cross-check must never make the approval mechanically ineligible** once this governance is active — this is the central difference from the retired mandatory gate.

**Decision — optional cross-check semantics.** When a cross-check is chosen (R2 only, Product Owner's choice, never automatic), it:

- independently inspects the exact candidate/repository authority — it must not inherit Review A's conclusion as ground truth;
- is advisory only, with no veto over the Product Owner's decision;
- is not an approval prerequisite under any circumstance;
- requires no persisted transcript/report;
- requires no standalone Review-B evidence file;
- requires no session/execution-ID persistence;
- requires no Mode A/Mode B bookkeeping for approval eligibility (ADR-031's execution-isolation evidence contract is not a precondition for this optional step);
- need not appear in the ADR/MANIFEST/CHANGELOG record of the decision.

If an optional cross-check finds a substantive defect, ordinary engineering judgment addresses the defect before the Product Owner decides — exactly as any other reviewer input would be handled. This practical response **must not** be written, interpreted, or operationalized as a hidden mandatory second-review gate; it is the same category of response as Review A itself finding a defect.

**Decision — historical evidence unaffected.** This ADR is prospective only, mirroring [ADR-031](./ADR-031.md) §10's own non-retroactivity principle. Every ADR/approval-gate decision already recorded under the mandatory-two-review gate keeps its existing reviewer evidence, Mode A/Mode B disposition, and approval validity exactly as recorded — nothing here reinterprets, invalidates, or requires re-review of prior decisions.

**Authority compatibility — `ADR-031` supersession scope.** `ADR-031`'s entire operative machinery (Principal vs Execution Identity distinction, Mode A/Mode B independence modes, the mandatory execution-isolation evidence contract) exists specifically to let a second review satisfy the *mandatory* two-review gate this ADR retires. Once this ADR's target model is activated (see Migration below), that machinery is no longer load-bearing for approval eligibility — an optional cross-check, when chosen, requires **no** Mode A/Mode B bookkeeping (see above). `ADR-031`'s file is never modified — not now, not later (Chapter 11 §11.3 forbids editing an Approved ADR; `ADR-031.md` stays byte-identical/immutable). Instead, `ADR-042` itself — via `depends_on: [ADR-031]`/`supersedes: [ADR-031]` (frontmatter) — carries the effect at its own future approval boundary, mirroring the same governance-relationship pattern already precedented by `ADR-016`→`ADR-015`, `ADR-014`→`ADR-003`, and `ADR-041`→`ADR-040` in this repository: supersession is a frontmatter/MANIFEST declaration, never a text edit into the superseded ADR's own file. `ADR-031`'s own definitions (§1-2, Principal Identity vs Execution Identity) are not declared incorrect and may still be referenced informationally if a Product Owner or reviewer wants to describe an optional cross-check's provenance — but no future governance artifact may treat that description as *required* bookkeeping for approval eligibility.

**Alternatives considered:**

1. **(1) Keep the mandatory two-review gate unconditionally (status quo).** Rejected — this is precisely the uniform ceremony the Product Owner decision direction identifies as disproportionate to actual risk for the majority of R0/R1 changes.
2. **(2) Keep the mandatory two-review gate only for R2 (auto-required, not optional).** Rejected — the Product Owner decision direction is explicit: "R2 MUST NOT automatically require the cross-check" and "Absence of a cross-check must never make the approval mechanically ineligible." An auto-required R2 gate would silently reintroduce the same ineligibility failure mode this ADR retires, just with a narrower trigger.
3. **(3) Retire Review A's mandatory status as well, leaving only Product Owner judgment.** Rejected — out of scope of the Product Owner decision direction, which explicitly keeps "Review A remains the single mandatory formal technical review." Removing all mandatory independent technical review would be a materially larger governance change than requested.
4. **(4) A formal risk-scoring system, review-evidence registry, or additional cross-check ceremony (forms, scoring, mandatory logging).** Rejected — the Product Owner decision direction explicitly instructs to "Avoid designing scoring systems, forms, evidence registries, or additional ceremony." R0/R1/R2 is a classification, not a scored/weighted system, and the optional cross-check is explicitly exempted from persistence/evidence requirements.

**Migration — this ADR only decides the target model; current governance remains controlling until a later, separate atomic activation boundary.** `ADR-042` itself must satisfy the **current** (pre-activation) mandatory two-independent-review gate before Product Owner approval — this ADR is not used to bootstrap its own rules, exactly as `ADR-031` itself was approved under the then-current distinct-principal-only gate before its own Mode B mechanism could be used. From the boundary this ADR is (if) Approved until a separate governed activation transaction, Chapter 0 §3/Chapter 11 §11.5/§11.9 **as they stand today** continue to govern the review gate unchanged — the mandatory two-review requirement remains fully in force.
